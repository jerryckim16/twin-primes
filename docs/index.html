Toward the twin prime conjecture: certificates, limits of the sieve, and a working programme

Jerry Kim

Version 0.9, 13 November 2026

This is a living working paper. It does not prove the twin prime conjecture, and it does not improve the best known bound on small gaps between primes. It records (i) the state of the problem as of the date above, (ii) what this project has verified for itself, by exact computation and by the Lean 4 kernel, now including the established bound H_1\le246 of Polymath8b, reproduced end to end through a certified lower bound for M_{50,1/25}, (iii) small results of our own: a no-go statement for a natural attempt to sharpen the pigeonhole step, on the plain and on the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex; a generating-function formula that makes certified computation with all symmetric test functions cheap; an upper bound showing that the \varepsilon-enlargement of Polymath8b cannot, by itself, reach any k\le29; numerical evidence that this upper bound is close to the limit of every dual weight depending only on t_m and t_1+\dots+t_k, and heuristic evidence that the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex stops at k=50; and, for the one-band hybrid supports of Stadlmann, a rigorous floor (no k\le28 at his level of distribution, whatever the caps), and an exact formula for his capped functionals, which gives a certified capped ratio kJ_T/I_T=4.0120>4 at k=49, computed in exact arithmetic by two independent programs: the variational step of Stadlmann’s H_1\le240 is thereby reproduced, while his new equidistribution estimates, on which 240 rests, have not been verified by us; the same computation confirms the variational criterion of Charton et al. at k=45 (4.0027>4) for the caps of their Table 3 but not, with our test function, for the different caps of their Lemma 9.1 (3.9737), and with those caps no polynomial in our 846-dimensional space reaches 3.99 (a ceiling certified in ball arithmetic, with weights checked exactly at a random point); optimising the test function on Stadlmann’s capped support itself, with exact Gram matrices obtained by running the exact evaluator backwards, raises his margin only from 4.0120 to 4.0124; for the support of Kintali at k=48 (claimed H_1\le236), our own test function of degree 22 gives 4.0061>4 (one exact program so far); and (iv) a programme of original directions that later versions will test. Every statement carries one of the labels Lean-checked, Computed, Proved here, Literature, Claimed (a preprint not yet independently verified), or Open.

The problem and the honest position

Write p_n for the n-th prime and H_1=\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_n). The twin prime conjecture is the statement H_1=2. It is Open.

A finite set \mathcal H=\{h_1<\dots<h_k\} of integers is admissible if for every prime p it misses at least one residue class modulo p. Let \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] be the statement that every admissible k-tuple has infinitely many translates n+\mathcal H containing at least two primes, and let H(k) be the least diameter h_k-h_1 of an admissible k-tuple. Then

\mathrm{DHL}[k,2]\ \Longrightarrow\ H_1\le H(k),\qquad(1)
and \mathrm{DHL}[2,2] applied to \{0,2\} is the twin prime conjecture. All progress on H_1 since 2013 consists of proving \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] for smaller k.

Why 2 is a different problem

Under the generalised Elliott–Halberstam conjecture the sieve gives H_1\le6, and Polymath8b showed that 6 cannot be improved by purely sieve-theoretic arguments: this is Selberg’s parity obstruction [14]. Lowering the bound (Track A below) and proving the conjecture (Track B) are therefore different tasks. No amount of success on Track A reaches 2.

What this project has verified for itself

The combinatorial half, checked by the Lean kernel

Our Lean development is written in core Lean 4, without Mathlib. It defines primality, the twin prime conjecture, the predicate BoundedGaps B (infinitely many pairs of distinct primes at distance at most B), admissibility, and \mathrm{DHL}[k,2]. It contains no sorry, and the only axioms it uses are propext and Quot.sound. The statement \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] is always a hypothesis, never an axiom.

  1. The twin prime conjecture is equivalent to BoundedGaps 2.

  2. If a finite Boolean check succeeds on a list \mathcal H (it misses a class modulo every m with 2\le m\le|\mathcal H|), then \mathcal H is admissible. The moduli p>|\mathcal H| are handled by a formal pigeonhole argument.

The sieve constant, with exact certificates

Let \mathcal{R}_k=\{t\in[0,1]^k:\ t_1+\dots+t_k\le1\} and, for square-integrable F supported on \mathcal{R}_k,

I_k(F)=\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}F^2,\qquad J_k^{(m)}(F)=\int\left(\int F\,dt_m\right)^{2}\prod_{i\ne m}dt_i,\qquad M_k=\sup_F\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}J_k^{(m)}(F)}{I_k(F)}.
Maynard’s theorem [10, Prop. 4.2] states that if the primes have level of distribution \theta and \theta M_k/2>1 then \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] holds. With Bombieri–Vinogradov (\theta<1/2) the condition is M_k>4.

We take F=\sum_{a+2b\le d}c_{a,b}(1-P_1)^aP_2^b with P_j=\sum_it_i^j, as in [10, §8]. All integrals reduce to

\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}(1-P_1)^AP_2^B\,dt=\frac{A!}{(k+A+2B)!}\,G_k(B),\qquad \sum_{B\ge0}G_k(B)\frac{x^B}{B!}=\left(\sum_{b\ge0}\frac{(2b)!}{b!}x^b\right)^{k},
which follows from the Dirichlet integral. The two quadratic forms are then exact rational matrices. We compute an approximate top eigenvector in floating point, round it to rationals, and evaluate the Rayleigh quotient in exact arithmetic; the last step is a rigorous lower bound whatever happened before it.

Theorem 2 (Computed, exact rational arithmetic). With F a polynomial in 1-P_1 and P_2 only,

M_{105}\ge4.00206976,\qquad M_{102}\ge4.00271343,\qquad M_{5}\ge2.00410541 .

The first value reproduces Maynard’s M_{105}>4 with d=11 (42 coefficients). The third gives \mathrm{DHL}[5,2] and H_1\le12 under the Elliott–Halberstam conjecture, again reproducing [10]. For d=11,15,19 the least k at which this family certifies M_k>4 is 105,102,102, and at k=101 it gives only 3.99931 (d=19): polynomials in P_1,P_2 alone stop at k=102.

All symmetric test functions

To go further we allow every symmetric polynomial. Since P_1,\dots,P_k generate the symmetric polynomials, the functions

(1-P_1)^a\,P^{b}:=(1-P_1)^a\prod_{j\ge2}P_j^{b_j},\qquad a+w(b)\le d,\quad w(b)=\sum_j jb_j,
span all symmetric polynomials of degree at most d (for k\ge d). The integrals have a closed form that we have not seen stated in this way (Proved here; possibly folklore).

Lemma 3. Let X_1,\dots,X_k be independent exponential random variables of mean 1 and E_k(b)=\mathbb E\prod_jP_j(X)^{b_j}. Then

\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}(1-P_1)^AP^{b}\,dt=\frac{A!}{(k+A+w(b))!}\,E_k(b), \qquad \sum_b E_k(b)\frac{s^b}{b!}=\left(\sum_{c}w(c)!\,\frac{s^c}{c!}\right)^{k},
where s^b=\prod_js_j^{b_j} and b!=\prod_jb_j!. In particular each E_k(b) is a positive integer, computed exactly by multiplying exponential generating functions.

Proof. For a monomial, the Dirichlet integral gives \int_{\mathcal{R}_k}(1-P_1)^At^\alpha\,dt=A!\,\alpha!/(k+A+|\alpha|)!, and \alpha!=\mathbb EX^\alpha. Summing over the monomials of P^b, all of degree w(b), gives the first identity. For the second, \mathbb E\exp(\sum_js_jP_j(X))=\left(\mathbb E\exp(\sum_js_jX_1^j)\right)^k by independence, and \mathbb E\exp(\sum_js_jX_1^j)=\sum_c\mathbb E X_1^{w(c)}\,s^c/c!=\sum_cw(c)!\,s^c/c!, as formal power series. ◻

The J-functional reduces to the same lemma in k-1 variables: with P_j=P_j'+t_k^j,

\int_0^{1-P_1'}(1-P_1)^aP^b\,dt_k=\sum_{e\le b}\binom{b}{e} \frac{a!\,w(e)!}{(a+w(e)+1)!}\,(1-P_1')^{a+w(e)+1}P'^{\,b-e}.
So both Gram matrices are exact rational matrices. We find an approximate top eigenvector by inverse iteration with a fixed shift above \frac{k}{k-1}\log k, which can only converge to the top eigenvalue. We take its floating-point midpoints as exact dyadic rationals and evaluate the Rayleigh quotient exactly. The working precision of the eigenvector matters only for speed. A separate script, code/verify_cert.py, rebuilds both Gram matrices from scratch and re-evaluates each stored coefficient vector in exact arithmetic.

Call the resulting constant M_{k,\varepsilon}. If \mathrm{EH}[\theta] holds with 1+\varepsilon<1/\theta and M_{k,\varepsilon}>2/\theta, then \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] holds. Bombieri–Vinogradov gives every \theta<1/2, so for every fixed \varepsilon<1 the condition is M_{k,\varepsilon}>4.

We extended our pipeline to this functional, following the route that [14, §7.2] describes in words. Put L=1+\varepsilon, \tau=1-\varepsilon and use the basis (L-P_1)^aP^b (b supported on even j). The following identities are elementary (Proved here): the substitution t=Ls and Lemma 3 give

\int_{L\mathcal{R}_k}(L-P_1)^AP^b\,dt=L^{k+A+w(b)}\,\frac{A!}{(k+A+w(b))!}\,E_k(b);
the inner integral over t_m\in[0,L-P_1'] is the formula displayed after Lemma 3 with 1-P_1' replaced by L-P_1'; and on \tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1} we write L-P_1'=2\varepsilon+(\tau-P_1'), so that
\int_{\tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1}}(L-P_1')^AP'^{\,B}=\sum_{i=0}^{A}\binom Ai(2\varepsilon)^{A-i}\, \tau^{k-1+i+w(B)}\,\frac{i!}{(k-1+i+w(B))!}\,E_{k-1}(B).
For rational \varepsilon both Gram matrices are again exact rational matrices. Results of our own: rigidity of the pigeonhole step

Every bound above ends with the same step: if the weighted average number of primes among n+h_1,\dots,n+h_k exceeds 1, two of them are prime, and they are at most h_k-h_1 apart. A natural hope is to do better with unequal weights. Fix D, and give each position a weight c_i\ge0. Call a set of positions D-separated if any two of its shifts differ by more than D, and let \alpha_D(c) be the largest total weight of a D-separated set. If no two primes among the n+h_i are within D, then \sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{n+h_i\text{ prime}}\le\alpha_D(c). Writing

M_k[c]=\sup_F\frac{\sum_{m}c_mJ_k^{(m)}(F)}{I_k(F)},
the sieve therefore yields H_1\le D as soon as \tfrac{\theta}{2}M_k[c]>\alpha_D(c). For c=(1,\dots,1) and D=h_k-h_1 this is the usual criterion. Could a cleverer c certify some D<h_k-h_1 that no sub-tuple certifies?

Proposition 8 (Proved here; possibly folklore). Let \omega be the largest number of shifts h_i contained in an interval of length D. Then for every c\ge0, not identically zero,

\frac{M_k[c]}{\alpha_D(c)}\le M_\omega .
So the weighted criterion never certifies more than the standard criterion applied to the best sub-tuple of diameter at most D.

Proof. Let G be the graph on \{1,\dots,k\} joining i\ne j when |h_i-h_j|\le D. Its stable sets are the D-separated sets and its cliques are the sets of shifts lying in an interval of length D. Normalise \alpha_D(c)=1, so c lies in

P=\{c\ge0:\ c(S)\le1\text{ for every stable set }S\}.
The graph G is a unit interval graph, hence perfect, and so is its complement by the perfect graph theorem [9]. For a perfect graph the polytope cut out by the clique inequalities is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of its stable sets [5]. Applying this to the complement of G gives
P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_K:\ K\text{ a clique of }G\}.

The map c\mapsto M_k[c] is a supremum of linear functionals, hence convex, and therefore M_k[c]\le\max_KM_k[\mathbf 1_K]. It remains to show M_k[\mathbf 1_K]\le M_{j} for |K|=j; this suffices because M_j is non-decreasing in j (adjoin a variable and concentrate F near its zero set). Write t=(s,u) with s the coordinates in K and u the others, put \sigma(u)=1-\sum u\ge0, and let F_u=F(\cdot,u), a function on the scaled simplex \sigma(u)\mathcal{R}_j. For m\in K we have J_k^{(m)}(F)=\int J_j^{(m)}(F_u)\,du, and I_k(F)=\int I_j(F_u)\,du. Rescaling F_u to \mathcal{R}_j multiplies I_j by \sigma^{j} and each J_j^{(m)} by \sigma^{j+1}, so

\sum_{m\in K}J_j^{(m)}(F_u)\ \le\ \sigma(u)\,M_j\,I_j(F_u)\ \le\ M_j\,I_j(F_u).
Integrating over u gives the claim. ◻

Remark 9 (Computed, consistency check for k=3). With unrestricted polynomials of degree 6 on \mathcal{R}_3 we find the lower bounds M_3[(1,1,1)]\ge1.6464, M_3[(1,1,0)]\ge1.3579 and M_3[(\tfrac12,1,\tfrac12)]\ge1.1604, against M_2=1/(1-W(1/e))=1.3859\ldots . The half-weighted functional is far below M_2, as the proposition requires.

The same rigidity on the enlarged simplex

The \varepsilon-enlarged simplex of Polymath8b [14, Thm. 3.12] takes F supported on (1+\varepsilon)\mathcal{R}_k and restricts the outer integral of each J^{(m)} to (1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{R}_{k-1}. Call the resulting constant M_{k,\varepsilon}. The criterion M_{k,\varepsilon}>2/\theta is valid in two cases (Literature): under \mathrm{EH}[\theta] when 1+\varepsilon<1/\theta, and under \mathrm{GEH}[\theta] when \varepsilon<1/(k-1). With Bombieri–Vinogradov the first case allows every \varepsilon<1, and Polymath8b’s H_1\le246 uses M_{50,1/25}>4.0043 [14, Thm. 3.13]. Unequal weights are no more useful there.

Proposition 10 (Proved here; possibly folklore). For 0\le\varepsilon<1 and every weight c\ge0, c\ne0, M_{k,\varepsilon}[c]/\alpha_D(c)\le M_{\omega,\varepsilon}, with equality for the indicator of a largest clique. Since the conditions on \varepsilon above do not get harder as k decreases, the weighted criterion never certifies more than the standard one for the best sub-tuple of diameter at most D.

Proof. As for Proposition 8, it suffices to show \sum_{m\in K}J_{k,\varepsilon}^{(m)}(F)\le M_{j,\varepsilon}I_k(F) for a clique K of size j, together with M_{j,\varepsilon}\le M_{\omega,\varepsilon}. Fix the coordinates u outside K, with |u|\le1-\varepsilon (other slices do not meet the J-region). The slice F_u is supported on (1+\varepsilon-|u|)\mathcal{R}_j, and its J-region is (1-\varepsilon-|u|)\mathcal{R}_{j-1}. Put \mu=(1+\varepsilon-|u|)/(1+\varepsilon)\le1 and H(x)=F_u(\mu x), which is supported on (1+\varepsilon)\mathcal{R}_j. Then I_j(F_u)=\mu^jI_j(H), and each slice J-term equals \mu^{j+1} times the integral of (\int H\,dx_m)^2 over |x'|\le(1-\varepsilon-|u|)/\mu. Since

\frac{(1-\varepsilon-|u|)(1+\varepsilon)}{1+\varepsilon-|u|}\le1-\varepsilon \iff -|u|(1+\varepsilon)\le-|u|(1-\varepsilon),
that region lies inside (1-\varepsilon)\mathcal{R}_{j-1}, so the slice contributes at most \mu^{j+1}M_{j,\varepsilon}I_j(H)=\mu M_{j,\varepsilon}I_j(F_u). For j=1 use Cauchy–Schwarz, which gives M_{1,\varepsilon}=1+\varepsilon. Integrate over u and use \mu\le1. Monotonicity in j follows by embedding: take F(s,u)=H(s/\mu_\delta)\mathbf 1_{[0,\delta]^{k-j}}(u) with \mu_\delta=1-(k-j)\delta/(1+\varepsilon) and let \delta\to0. ◻

Slicing naively gives each slice its own parameter \varepsilon'=\varepsilon/(1-|u|)\ge\varepsilon, which would only bound the weighted constant by \sup_{\varepsilon'\ge\varepsilon}M_{j,\varepsilon'}, a weaker statement. The single rescaling by \mu above avoids that loss.

Results of our own: how far the \varepsilon-enlargement alone can go

For the plain simplex the bound M_k\le\frac{k}{k-1}\log k shows where the method stops (k\ge51). This section starts the same analysis for the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex. For 0<\tau\le L put

M_k(L,\tau)=\sup_F\frac{\sum_{m=1}^k\int_{\tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1}}\left(\int_0^\infty F\,dt_m\right)^2\prod_{i\ne m}dt_i}{\int F^2},
the supremum over square-integrable F\ne0 supported on L\mathcal{R}_k. Then M_{k,\varepsilon}=M_k(1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon) and M_k=M_k(1,1). The quantity M_k(L,\tau) is non-decreasing in L and in \tau, satisfies M_k(cL,c\tau)=c\,M_k(L,\tau), and is non-decreasing in k by the embedding used in the proof of Proposition 10. Write S=t_1+\dots+t_k and call the index m active at t if S-t_m\le\tau.

Lemma 13 (Proved here; it is [14, Lemma 6.1] with the region of the marginals kept). Let \psi_1,\dots,\psi_k be measurable functions on L\mathcal{R}_k, positive almost everywhere, such that for almost every t'\in\tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1} and every m

\int_0^{L-|t'|}\frac{du}{\psi_m(t',u)}\le1,
where (t',u) is the point whose m-th coordinate is u and whose other coordinates are t'. Then
M_k(L,\tau)\le\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{t\in L\mathcal{R}_k}\ \sum_{m\text{ active at }t}\psi_m(t).

Proof. For t'\in\tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1} the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\left(\int F\,dt_m\right)^2\le\int\frac{dt_m}{\psi_m}\cdot\int\psi_mF^2\,dt_m\le\int\psi_mF^2\,dt_m .
Integrating over t'\in\tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1} gives the integral of \psi_mF^2 over the set where m is active. Sum over m. ◻

Proposition 14 (Proved here; elementary, possibly folklore). For k\ge2 and 0\le\varepsilon<1,

M_{k,\varepsilon}\le(1+\varepsilon)M_k\le(1+\varepsilon)\frac{k}{k-1}\log k .
Conversely, for 0\le\varepsilon\le M_k/(4k),
M_{k,\varepsilon}\ge(1+\varepsilon)M_k-\frac{64k^2}{M_k}\,\varepsilon^2 .
In particular \lim_{\varepsilon\to0^+}(M_{k,\varepsilon}-M_k)/\varepsilon=M_k.

Proof. By monotonicity in \tau and scaling, M_k(1+\varepsilon,1-\varepsilon)\le M_k(1+\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon)=(1+\varepsilon)M_k, and M_k\le\frac{k}{k-1}\log k is [14, Cor. 6.4].

For the lower bound, scaling gives M_{k,\varepsilon}=(1+\varepsilon)M_k(1,\rho) with \rho=(1-\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon). Put \delta=k(1-\rho) and let F on \mathcal{R}_k satisfy \sum_mJ_k^{(m)}(F)\ge(M_k-\eta)I_k(F). Split F=F_1+F_2 with F_2=F\mathbf 1_{S>\rho}, and let x=I_k(F_2)/I_k(F). Two facts follow from the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality on segments of length at most 1-\rho. First, a fiber of F_2 has length at most 1-\rho, so \sum_mJ_k^{(m)}(F_2)\le\delta\,I_k(F_2). Second, a fiber over t' with |t'|>\rho lies in \{S>\rho\} and has length at most 1-\rho, so restricting the marginals of F to \rho\mathcal{R}_{k-1} loses at most \delta\,I_k(F_2). By the triangle inequality for F\mapsto(\int F\,dt_m)_{m\le k} and the first fact,

M_k-\eta\le\left(\sqrt{M_k(1-x)}+\sqrt{\delta x}\right)^2\le M_k(1-x)+2\sqrt{M_k\delta x}+\delta x .
This is a quadratic inequality in \sqrt x. If \delta\le M_k/2 it gives x\le(16\delta+8\eta)/M_k. By the second fact the quotient of F for M_k(1,\rho) is at least M_k-\eta-\delta x. Letting \eta\to0 we get M_k(1,\rho)\ge M_k-16\delta^2/M_k. Finally \delta=2k\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon), and \delta\le M_k/2 when \varepsilon\le M_k/(4k). ◻

So for fixed k the factor 1+\varepsilon is exact to first order, and no dual argument can improve it there. Any saving is an effect of large k: it needs \varepsilon of size about 1/k or more, where the shell \rho<\sigma\le1 carries most of the mass. The first inequality already restricts the parameter: with Bombieri–Vinogradov the criterion M_{k,\varepsilon}>4 forces \varepsilon>\varepsilon_0(k)=\frac{4(k-1)}{k\log k}-1 (Computed):

Theorem 15 (Proved here for the inequality; Computed, interval arithmetic, for the numerical statement). Let k\ge2, 0<\tau<s_1\le L and a\ge s_1. Then

M_k(L,\tau)\le\sup_{0\le\sigma\le\tau}W(\sigma),\qquad W(\sigma)=\frac{ka}{k-1}\log\left(1+\frac{(k-1)(s_1-\sigma)}{a-\sigma}\right)+L-s_1+\sigma\log\frac{L-\sigma}{s_1-\sigma}.
Consequently M_{k,\varepsilon}<3.99 for every k\le29 and every 0\le\varepsilon<1. In particular the criterion of [14, Thm. 3.12] on the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex cannot give \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] for any k\le29 from the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, nor from any level of distribution \theta\le0.501, whatever the value of \varepsilon.

Proof. Fix \Lambda>0, put \lambda=\Lambda/(ka) and take the two-zone weight

\psi_m(t)=\lambda\,(a-S+kt_m)\quad(S\le s_1),\qquad \psi_m(t)=\Lambda\,\frac{t_m}{S}\quad(S>s_1).
It is positive wherever t_m>0. Where S\le s_1\le a every term is non-negative, so the sum over the active indices is at most the sum over all indices, which is \lambda(k(a-S)+kS)=\Lambda. Where S>s_1 the sum over the active indices is at most \Lambda\sum_mt_m/S=\Lambda. On the fiber over t' with |t'|=\sigma\le\tau we have S=\sigma+u, and
\int_0^{s_1-\sigma}\frac{du}{\lambda(a-\sigma+(k-1)u)}+\int_{s_1-\sigma}^{L-\sigma}\frac{(\sigma+u)\,du}{\Lambda u}=\frac{W(\sigma)}{\Lambda}.
Lemma 13 with \Lambda=\sup W gives the inequality.

For the numerical statement it suffices to treat k=29. For \varepsilon\le143/1000, Proposition 14 gives M_{29,\varepsilon}\le1.143\cdot\frac{29}{28}\log29<3.9863. The interval [143/1000,1] is cut into 857 intervals [e,e'] of length 1/1000. For each one the file code/cert_floor_k29.json lists rational a and s_1 with \sup W<3.99 for (L,\tau)=(1+e',1-e); by monotonicity in L and \tau this covers every \varepsilon\in[e,e']. The supremum over \sigma is bounded rigorously: the first logarithm is non-increasing in \sigma (because a\ge s_1) and the last term is non-decreasing, so on a piece [x,y] we bound W by the first term at x plus the last at y, in ball arithmetic at rational points, bisecting until the bound is below 3.99. The script is code/dual_eps_cert.py; a second script, code/check_floor_cert.py, re-checks the certificate with a different interval library. The largest value of \sup W over the 857 intervals is 3.9772 (floating point). ◻

Remark 16 (what was known, and a caution). Polymath8b state the uniform bound M_{k,\varepsilon}\le\frac{k}{k-1}\log(2k-1) for 0\le\varepsilon<1 [14, Prop. 6.5], and remark that the bounds for M_{k,\varepsilon} “do not appear to be as sharp”. That bound is below 4 exactly for k\le23, so Theorem 15 moves this floor from 24 to 30. It does not use their bound. We note, with caution, that we could not complete the last step of the proof of [14, Prop. 6.5] as we read it: the weights 1-S+kt_m are summed, and for S>1 their sum over the active indices can exceed k (at t=(s,0,\dots,0) with 1<s\le1+\varepsilon only the first index is active and its weight is 1+(k-1)s). Our reconstruction gives the constant \frac{k}{k-1}+\varepsilon in place of \frac{k}{k-1}. For version 0.4 we re-read the proof in the current arXiv text: after the Cauchy–Schwarz step it says only “Integrating in t_1,\dots,t_{i-1}, t_{i+1},\dots,t_k and summing in i, we obtain the claim.” The weights 1-S+kt_i of the inactive indices can be negative when S>1, and dropping them is what lets the sum exceed k; it is at most k+(k-1)(S-1)\le k+(k-1)\varepsilon, which gives our constant. So the argument as printed proves M_{k,\varepsilon}\le\left(\frac{k}{k-1}+\varepsilon\right)\log(2k-1). Whether the stated inequality is nevertheless true is Open; we have not seen the journal version, and we may be missing an intended step. A brief search (the paper [14] and the Polymath wiki page on the variational problem) found no sharper published upper bound for M_{k,\varepsilon}; the search was not exhaustive, and Theorem 15 has not been reviewed by anyone outside this project.

Remark 18 (a weight that did not work). We also tried the weight \psi_m=\frac{\beta}{n}(a-S_A+nt_m), where n is the number of active indices at t and S_A the sum of the active coordinates. Its sum over the active indices is exactly \beta a everywhere, so the bound is a times the largest fiber integral. Evaluating the fiber integral exactly on the fibers over t'=(x,\dots,x,0,\dots,0) (Computed, floating point, k=50, \varepsilon=1/25, every a\in[1.05,3] on a grid of step 0.01, script code/dual_eps.py remark16) shows that this weight cannot give less than 4.178, which is worse than the 4.1515 from scaling. The binding fiber has a single non-zero coordinate x\approx2\varepsilon: there the zero coordinates become inactive while n is still large, and the normalisation by n wastes the slack. A dual bound that is sharper for small \varepsilon must treat the small coordinates near such sparse points differently. This is Open. (An earlier draft of this remark, version 0.3 as first circulated, reported a weaker obstruction, 4.146, from fibers with about ten non-zero coordinates; the exact fiber evaluation above supersedes it.)

The limit of weights that see only t_m and S (version 0.4)

Every weight used so far has the form \psi_m(t)=\Psi(S-t_m,t_m): Polymath8b’s \lambda(a-S+kt_m) and both zones of Theorem 15. Call this family \mathcal W. For weights in \mathcal W the problem “minimise the bound of Lemma 13” is convex: each fiber condition \int du/\Psi\le1 is convex in \Psi, and each point t gives a linear condition \sum_{m\text{ active}}\Psi(S-t_m,t_m)\le\Lambda. The support and the criterion

The following is Literature, read in [18, Def. 1, Def. 5, Prop. 1]. For \delta>0, -\varepsilon=A_0<A_1<\dots<A_n<\frac12-\varepsilon and caps \delta<B_{j,m}\le B_{j,m+1}\le B_{j,m}+\delta, the support T_k(\delta,\underline A,\underline B,\varepsilon) is the set of t\in[0,1]^k such that, for the j with \sum_it_i\in[A_{j-1}+\varepsilon,A_j+\varepsilon), one has \sum_{i\in I}t_i\le B_{j,|I|} where I=\{i:t_i>\delta\} (the “large” coordinates). With one band (n=1), the functional J(F) of [18] is the integral of \left(\int F\,dt_k\right)^2 over t_1+\dots+t_{k-1}\le A_1-\varepsilon (our reduction of the general definition), I(F)=\int_{T_k}F^2, and the criterion is k(1-c_1)J(F)-kc_2K(F)>I(F) for a symmetric F supported on T_k, where c_1,c_2\ge0 come from the minorant and K(F)\ge0. Stadlmann’s choice is k=49, \varepsilon=0.0075, A_1=0.253, \delta=0.028, B_{1,1}=B_{1,2}=0.15, B_{1,m}=0.17 (m\ge3) and c_1=c_2=0; that of [3] is k=45, \varepsilon=1/125, A_1=257/1000, \delta=41/2500 with caps listed in their Table 3. Both use one band. The equidistribution estimates behind the criterion are Claimed; we have not checked them.

In the Polymath scaling u=4t the support lies in L\mathcal{R}_k with L=4(A_1+\varepsilon), the marginals are integrated over \tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1} with \tau=4(A_1-\varepsilon), and the criterion with c_1=c_2=0 reads kJ/I>4. Write s=(L+\tau)/2=4A_1 and e=(L-\tau)/(L+\tau)=\varepsilon/A_1; the scaling M_k(cL,c\tau)=cM_k(L,\tau) of Section 4 gives

M_k(L,\tau)=s\,M_k(1+e,1-e)=4A_1\,M_{k,\varepsilon/A_1}.\qquad(2)
For Stadlmann, (L,\tau)=(1.042,0.982), s=253/250, e=15/506; for [3], (L,\tau)=(1.06,0.996), s=257/250, e=8/257.

With the caps

For F=P\,1_T with P a polynomial, write I(P), J(P) for the cap-free functionals on L\mathcal{R}_k (marginals over \tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1}) and V=L\mathcal{R}_k\setminus T (Polymath scaling; g=4\delta, C_m=4B_{1,m}).

Proposition 23 (Proved here; elementary). Let R_m(g,c)=\{u\in L\mathcal{R}_k:\ u_1,\dots,u_m>g,\ u_1+\dots+u_m\ge c\}, put c_m=C_m for m\le6 and c_7=7g. Then, with

E(P)=\sum_{m=1}^{7}\binom km\int_{R_m(g,c_m)}P^2 ,
one has I_T(F)\le I(P) and, for every 0<\eta<1,
J_T(F)\ \ge\ \max\left(J(P)-2\sqrt{J(P)\,L\,E(P)},\ (1-\eta)J(P)-(\eta^{-1}-1)L\,E(P)\right).

Proof. A point of V has a large set I with \sum_{i\in I}u_i>C_{|I|}. If |I|\le6 it lies in a copy of R_{|I|}(g,c_{|I|}); if |I|\ge7, any seven of its large coordinates exceed g and sum to more than 7g, so it lies in a copy of R_7(g,c_7). By symmetry of P^2, \int_VP^2\le E(P). On the fiber over u' write a=\int P\,du_k and b=\int P\,1_V\,du_k, so that the marginal of F is a-b. Cauchy–Schwarz gives b^2\le L\int P^21_V\,du_k, hence \int b^2\le L\,E(P). Then (a-b)^2\ge a^2-2|a||b| with Cauchy–Schwarz once more, or (a-b)^2\ge(1-\eta)a^2-(\eta^{-1}-1)b^2. Finally I_T(F)=\int_TP^2\le I(P). ◻

The integrals over R_m(g,c) are again exact rational numbers for the basis (L-P_1)^aP^b (Proved here; code/caps_exact.py). Write the first m coordinates as g+z. Integrating out the other k-m coordinates with Lemma 3 leaves powers of L-mg-|z|; the shifted power sums are P_j(g+z)=\sum_l\binom jlg^{j-l}P_l(z) with P_0=m and P_1=|z|; at fixed r=|z| the integral of \prod_lP_l(z)^{f_l} is r^{m-1+w(f)}E_m(f)/(m-1+w(f))! (now with odd parts as well); and the remaining integral in r over [\max(c-mg,0),L-mg] is elementary. The code reproduces Lemma 3 exactly when g=c=0, and agrees with direct symbolic integration (sympy) for k=3, m=1,2, g=1/10.

Since all three quantities are exact quadratic forms in the coefficients of P, we choose P as the top eigenvector of (1-\eta)J-(\eta^{-1}-1)L\,G_E against I, where G_E is the Gram matrix of E, and evaluate both bounds exactly. The penalty works: at degree 16 it lowers E/I from 3.2\cdot10^{-4} (for the cap-free optimiser) to 3.4\cdot10^{-7}.

The capped functionals exactly (version 0.6)

Version 0.5 bounded J_T(P\,1_T) through Proposition 23, and the Cauchy–Schwarz step there turned out to be the whole difficulty. We now compute I_T(P\,1_T) and J_T(P\,1_T) exactly. Notation as above: u in the Polymath scaling, R=L-|u'| on the fiber over u'\in\mathbb R_{\ge0}^{k-1}, I(u)=\{i:u_i>g\}, m(u)=|I(u)|, s(u)=\sum_{i\in I(u)}u_i, and Q(u',x)=\int_0^xP(u',t)\,dt.

Proposition 25 (Proved here; elementary). Assume C_{m+1}\le C_m+g for all m (this is Stadlmann’s condition B_{j,m+1}\le B_{j,m}+\delta). Call u' admissible if s(u')\le C_{m(u')}. Then:

  1. the fiber \{t\ge0:(u',t)\in T\} is empty if u' is not admissible, and otherwise equals [0,U(u')] with U=\min\left(R,\max(g,\,C_{m(u')+1}-s(u'))\right);

  2. one has I_T(P\,1_T)=I(P)-\int_VP^2 and

    J_T(P\,1_T)=J(P)-\mathrm{Corr}_1-\mathrm{Corr}_2,
    where \mathrm{Corr}_1=\int Q(u',R)^2 over the non-admissible u' with |u'|\le\tau, and \mathrm{Corr}_2=\int\left(Q(u',R)^2-Q(u',U)^2\right) over the admissible u' with U<R;

  3. each of the three correction integrals is a finite signed sum, over the size m of the large set and over j\ge0 further coordinates forced above g (inclusion–exclusion for “all other coordinates are at most g”, with weight (-1)^j\binom nm\binom{n-m}{j}), of integrals over polygons in the plane of r_A=s-mg and \rho=|u|-s-jg. On each polygon the integrand, after the shape integrals of Lemma 3 and of Proposition 23, is a polynomial in (r_A,\rho), so every term is an exact rational number.

For Stadlmann’s parameters every correction piece has m\le9 and j\le3.

Proof. (1) If t\le g the large set of (u',t) is I(u'), so (u',t)\in T iff u' is admissible and |u'|+t\le L. If t>g the large set is I(u')\cup\{k\} and the condition is s(u')+t\le C_{m(u')+1}; since C_{m+1}\le C_m+g, this condition with t>g implies s(u')<C_{m(u')}, so a non-admissible u' has an empty fiber. Hence for admissible u' the fiber is [0,\min(R,g)]\cup(g,\min(R,C_{m+1}-s)], which is [0,U]. (2) F=P\,1_T has marginal Q(u',U) on admissible u' and 0 otherwise, while the cap-free marginal is Q(u',R); subtract. (3) By symmetry, integrate over the points whose large set is exactly A=\{1,\dots,m\} and multiply by \binom nm; the condition that the remaining coordinates are at most g is \prod(1-1_{u_i>g}), expanded. On such a piece put z=u-g on the m+j forced coordinates; at fixed r_A=|z_A| and \rho=|z_B|+|y| (y the free coordinates) the integral of a product of power sums is a sum over splits of the product of the A-group density of Proposition 23 and the convolution of the j shifted coordinates with the free ones, which is again of Dirichlet type. The integrand (P^2, Q(R)^2, Q(g)^2 or Q(x)^2 with x=C_{m+1}-s) is a polynomial in R and x, both affine in (r_A,\rho). The regions are: r_A\ge C_m-mg and r_A+\rho\le const (the non-admissible pieces); r_A\ge C_{m+1}-(m+1)g and R>g (where U=g); and the rectangle r_A<C_{m+1}-(m+1)g, \rho<L-C_{m+1}-jg (where U=C_{m+1}-s). In every piece the large set carries at least C_1=0.6 or the free mass is below L-C_1, so jg<0.442, j\le3; and mg<L gives m\le9. Both counts are Lean-checked (stadlmann_extra_le_three, stadlmann_large_count). ◻

Up to version 0.7 every capped value came from a polynomial optimised without the caps and then truncated to T. Both capped functionals are quadratic forms in the coefficients c of P: I_T(P\,1_T)=c^{\top}G_Ic and J_T(P\,1_T)=c^{\top}G_Jc.

Proposition 31 (Proved here; elementary). In the direct method of code/capped_check.py, I_T is a linear function of the coefficient products \kappa_I[F](y)=\sum_{F_1+F_2=F}p_{F_1}(y)p_{F_2}(y) and J_T a linear function of \kappa_J[F](y,v)=\sum_{F_1+F_2=F}q_{F_1}q_{F_2}, where P=\sum_Fp_F(y)\prod_jP_j^{F_j}, y=L-P_1, and q_F(y,v) are the coefficients of \int_0^vP\,dt. Running the chain of exact linear maps of that program backwards gives exact weights w_I[F][\alpha]=\partial I_T/\partial\kappa_I[F][y^\alpha] and w_J[F][(b,\gamma)]=\partial J_T/\partial\kappa_J[F][y^bv^\gamma], and then G_I[t_1,t_2]=w_I[F_1+F_2][a_1+a_2] for basis terms t_i=(L-P_1)^{a_i}\prod_jP_j^{F_{i,j}}, and G_J=Q^{\top}HQ with H[(F_1,b_1,\gamma_1),(F_2,b_2,\gamma_2)]=w_J[F_1+F_2][(b_1+b_2,\gamma_1+\gamma_2)] and Q the (sparse, explicit) map from basis terms to the q_F.

The reverse pass costs about one forward evaluation (code/capped_gram.py). Its self-test is exact: for the input polynomial, \sum\langle\kappa_I,w_I\rangle and \sum\langle\kappa_J,w_J\rangle equal the I_T and J_T of capped_check.py as rationals (passed at k=12, at k=49 with the polynomial of Theorem 26, and at k=45 with the polynomial and caps of Proposition 33). G_I is exact; G_J is assembled in fixed point with an explicit entrywise error bound. The matrices are only used to find a vector, or, with that error bound, to certify that \sigma G_I-kG_J is positive definite (recursive Schur complements in ball arithmetic), i.e. that no P in the whole test space exceeds \sigma; every capped value stated as a certificate is recomputed from the rounded vector by the exact evaluators.

Proposition 32 (Computed, exact rational arithmetic, two programs). For Stadlmann’s support and parameters (k=49, as in Theorem 26) let P^\ast be the top generalised eigenvector of (k\,G_J,G_I) in the same 707-dimensional space (even power sums, degree 20), rounded to 400 significant bits per coefficient. Then

\frac{k\,J_T(P^\ast1_T)}{I_T(P^\ast1_T)}=4.0124198475\ldots\ >\ 4 .

Files: code/cap6/P_opt49_D20r_u.json (vector), code/cap6/check_opt49_D20r.json (capped_check.py, 7 minutes on two cores), and code/cap6/res_opt49_D20r.json (capped_exact.py, 60 minutes on two cores); the two programs agree on I_T and J_T as exact rationals. The unrounded vector gives the same value to twelve digits (check_opt49_D20.json). The cap-free value of P^\ast is 4.0158303, against 4.0161761 for the cap-free optimiser.

So optimising on T regains only 0.00038 of the 0.0041 that truncation cost (about 9\%): in this test space the margin of the variational step of [18] is 0.0124, and the caps cost 0.0038 even for the best polynomial of degree 20.

Proposition 33 (Computed, ball arithmetic; versions 0.8–0.9). Let T' be the support of [3] at k=45 with the caps of their Lemma 9.1 (C_1=C_2=31/50, C_m=17/25 for m\ge3; L=53/50, \tau=249/250, g=41/625), and let V be our 846-dimensional space (even power sums times powers of L-P_1, degree \le21). Then 3.99\,G_I-45\,G_J is positive definite, so

\frac{45\,J_{T'}(P\,1_{T'})}{I_{T'}(P\,1_{T'})}<3.99<4\qquad\text{for every }0\ne P\in V .

The weights w_I,w_J of Proposition 31 were computed exactly (88 minutes on two cores, version 0.8). G_J was assembled with absolute error at most 2^{-P} per weight, and the resulting entrywise error bound was put into the balls. Positive definiteness was certified by recursive Schur complements in ball arithmetic: for \sigma=4 at P=5000 bits with halving blocks (version 0.8), and again for \sigma=4 and \sigma=3.99 at P=6500 bits with blocks split 1:2 (version 0.9; code/capped_gram.py –ceiling 4/1,3990/1000 –split 1/3, log runs/gram45_lemma91_recheck.log).

How the weights were checked. Both c\mapsto c^{\top}G_Ic and the map c\mapsto I_{T'} computed by capped_check.py are polynomials of degree 2 in the 846 coefficients, and likewise for J. If they differed, their difference would be a nonzero polynomial of degree at most 2. By the Schwartz–Zippel lemma it then vanishes at a uniformly random point of S^{846}, |S|=2^{32}+1, with probability at most 2/|S|<2^{-31}. At the random integer vector code/cap6/P_rand45_lemma91_u.json (seed recorded in the file) the weights reproduce I_{T'} and J_{T'} of capped_check.py exactly (runs/gram45_rand_selftest.log, code/cap6/check_rand45_lemma91.json). The ball forms c^{\top}G_Ic and c^{\top}G_Jc contain these exact values. So the proposition rests on capped_check.py (which agrees exactly with the independent capped_exact.py at Stadlmann’s and at Charton et al.’s Table 3 parameters, Theorem 26 and Proposition 29) up to a failure probability below 2^{-31} for each functional. The same random point has not yet been evaluated by capped_exact.py. That run needs about four hours here and was stopped after 138 of its pieces, so the cross-check of capped_check.py itself at these caps is still to come.

The optimum. Inverse iteration in V converges to 45J_{T'}/I_{T'}=3.98555 (code/cap6/P_opt45_lemma91_D21r_u.json, the eigenvector rounded to 400 bits; a floating-point value from the Gram matrices, not re-certified exactly). So in V the truncated cap-free vector (3.97369) is beaten by 0.0119, the best possible lies in [3.98555,\,3.99), and 4 is out of reach.

Since [3] also report 846 coefficients, this suggests that the caps of Lemma 9.1 cannot give their 4.00438 in a space of this size, and that the value they report belongs to the caps of their Table 3, for which our truncated vector already gives 4.0027 (Proposition 29). We have not checked that their space is the same as ours (only the dimension is equal), so this is a located discrepancy for the authors or an expert reader, not a refutation.

Remark 34 (a failed prediction, our own). Before the run we registered a prediction (notes/session8-prediction.md). At k=49, optimising on T had regained only 9\% of what truncation cost (0.00038 of 0.0041). We guessed that re-optimisation is a second-order effect and would again regain about a tenth, giving 3.977–3.981 here. It regains 31\% (0.0119 of 0.0386), and the optimum 3.98555 lies outside the predicted range. A pure second-order picture, with gain about \mathrm{loss}^2/\mathrm{gap} and the gap fitted at k=49, would instead predict a gain larger than the loss itself. Neither guess is right: the fraction regained grows with the size of the truncation loss, but not quadratically. The heuristic is ruled out as a quantitative tool.

A floor for one-band hybrid supports (our own)

Polymath8b’s \varepsilon-simplex is the case A_1=1/4 without caps, so the following extends Theorem 15 to every one-band support of Stadlmann’s type.

Theorem 36 (Proved here for the reduction; Computed, interval arithmetic, for the table). Let T=T_k(\delta,(-\varepsilon,A_1),\underline B,\varepsilon) be a one-band support with \varepsilon\ge0 and A_1\le\frac14+\omega, with any \delta and any caps. If the criterion k(1-c_1)J(F)-kc_2K(F)>I(F) holds for some F supported on T, then M_k\left(4(\tfrac14+\omega+\varepsilon),4(\tfrac14+\omega-\varepsilon)\right)>4. This is false, for every 0\le\varepsilon<\frac14+\omega, in each of the following cases:

\omega 0 3/1000 1/300 7/1000 1/68
no k with k\le29 k\le28 k\le28 k\le26 k\le22

So, whatever \delta, the caps and the minorant, a one-band support cannot give \mathrm{DHL}[k,2] for k\le28 at Stadlmann’s level (\omega=0.003; the helper’s reconstruction of his conditions gives \omega\le0.0032), for k\le26 at the level \omega=0.007 of [3], and for k\le22 whenever \omega\le1/68, a range containing every \omega that satisfies the Type I condition 68\omega+14\delta<1 of [18, Lemma 5].

Proof. As c_1,c_2\ge0 and K(F)\ge0, the criterion implies kJ(F)>I(F). In the scaling u=4t, F becomes a function supported on L\mathcal{R}_k (because T\subseteq\{\sum t_i\le A_1+\varepsilon\}), the ratio is multiplied by 4, and J is the marginal functional over \tau\mathcal{R}_{k-1}; so 4<M_k(4(A_1+\varepsilon),4(A_1-\varepsilon)), and monotonicity in L and \tau replaces A_1 by \frac14+\omega. (For \varepsilon<0 one has \tau>L and the quantity is at most LM_k, which is covered by the case \varepsilon=0 below.) Monotonicity in k reduces each entry to the largest k shown. For the numerical part, put A=\frac14+\omega. When 4(A+\varepsilon)\frac{k}{k-1}\log k<4 we use M_k(L,\tau)\le M_k(L,L)=LM_k\le L\frac{k}{k-1}\log k [14, Cor. 6.4]. The remaining range of \varepsilon, up to A, is cut into intervals of length at most 1/2000; on [e,e'] we take (L,\tau)=(4(A+e'),4(A-e)), which dominates by monotonicity, and certify \sup_\sigma W(\sigma)<4 with rational (a,s_1) exactly as in the proof of Theorem 15. The certificates are code/cert_floor_hybrid_k*_w*.json, written by code/floor_hybrid.py in ball arithmetic (python-flint) and re-checked by the same script with –check in a different interval library (mpmath.iv). ◻

Remark 37 (how sharp). For each \omega in the table the same weight fails at the next k: the floating-point \sup W reaches 4 on some interval of \varepsilon (for example k=27, \omega=0.007, near \varepsilon\approx0.0548). So the table shows the reach of this weight, not of the method; the true floor is presumably much higher, as Proposition 22 suggests (it shows that the cap-free quantity already exceeds 4 at k=49 and k=45 for the two parameter sets). The theorem ignores the caps entirely, which only shrink the support; a floor that sees the caps is the natural next step. It also does not apply to supports with several bands, where J contains cross terms between bands, nor to the constructions behind 186 and 182, which we have not read in enough detail.

Programme

Track A: lowering the bound

  1. Climb the ladder. The certified pipeline now stands at k=50 (H_1\le246, the established bound, via the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex). Stadlmann’s region (k=49, 240): version 0.6 reproduces the variational step exactly (Theorem 26, Corollary 27), so the project’s bound is 240 conditionally on the unverified equidistribution estimates of [18]. For 212 (k=45, [3]) version 0.7 confirms the variational criterion for the caps of their Table 3 (Proposition 29); before the project’s bound can move, the discrepancy between their Table 3 and Lemma 9.1 must be resolved; version 0.8 has the tool for optimising on the capped support (Proposition 31), and for the caps of Lemma 9.1 no polynomial in our 846-dimensional space reaches 3.99 (Proposition 33); so the draft’s value must belong to its Table 3 caps. For 236 (k=48) the variational criterion of [8] holds with our own degree-22 vector (Proposition 35); a second exact program is the next step, and the analytic input remains Claimed. Then 186 and 182.

  2. Floor of the method (our main original direction). For the plain simplex the dual bound M_k\le\frac{k}{k-1}\log k shows where the method stops (k\ge51). For the \varepsilon-enlarged simplex Theorem 15 gives k\ge30, which is still far from the expected truth near 50; Section 4 explains what a sharper weight must do, and Proposition 19 indicates that it must depend on more of t than t_m and S (a Collatz–Wielandt weight \psi_m=\int F\,dt_m/F built from a good positive test function F is the natural candidate). For the one-band hybrid supports of [18, 3], Theorem 36 gives the first such bound we know of (k\ge29 at Stadlmann’s level, k\ge27 at that of [3]), still far from 49 and 45; it ignores the caps. A rigorous dual certificate there would say whether 182 is close to the limit of the present inputs or far from it, and which input (which Type I/II/III range) is binding.

  3. Which rung is cheap. From Table 1, the gain per unit of k below the claimed frontier is 182\to176\to168\to162\to158 for k=39,\dots,35: the steps 39\to38 and 38\to37 are worth the most.

Problem 38 (Open; partly answered by Theorem 36 for one band, without using the caps). Give a rigorous upper bound for the variational quantity of [18] as a function of k and of the admissible exponents, and deduce the least k the hybrid method can reach with the currently proved estimates.

Track B: the parity barrier

No sieve of the above kind can distinguish integers with an even number of prime factors from those with an odd number. Known ways around the barrier all inject bilinear or structural information. We will study, one per session, what each would need in order to say something about twin primes: Chen’s theorem [4]; Bombieri’s asymptotic sieve [2]; the parity-breaking sieve of Friedlander and Iwaniec [6]; Heath-Brown’s theorem that Siegel zeros would imply the conjecture [7]; the function-field proof of Sawin and Shusterman [16]; and the entropy-based results on correlations of multiplicative functions [19].

Problem 39 (Open, to be made precise in Lean). State, as a single explicit hypothesis on bilinear sums over n(n+2), the weakest known input that implies the twin prime conjecture, and formalise the implication.

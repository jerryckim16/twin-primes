The paper

Toward the twin prime conjecture: certificates, limits of the sieve, and a working programme

Jerry Kim

Version 0.2, 12 November 2026

This is a living working paper. It does not prove the twin prime conjecture, and it does not improve the best known bound on small gaps between primes. It records (i) the state of the problem as of the date above, (ii) what this project has verified for itself, by exact computation and by the Lean 4 kernel, (iii) small results of our own: a no-go statement for a natural attempt to sharpen the pigeonhole step, on the plain and on the ε\varepsilon-enlarged simplex, and a generating-function formula that makes certified computation with all symmetric test functions cheap, and (iv) a programme of original directions that later versions will test. Every statement carries one of the labels Lean-checked, Computed, Proved here, Literature, Claimed (a preprint not yet independently verified), or Open.

The problem and the honest position

Write pnp_n for the nn-th prime and H1=liminfn→∞(pn+1−pn)H_1=\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_n). The twin prime conjecture is the statement H1=2H_1=2. It is Open.

A finite set ℋ={h1<…<hk}\mathcal H=\{h_1<\dots<h_k\} of integers is admissible if for every prime pp it misses at least one residue class modulo pp. Let DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] be the statement that every admissible kk-tuple has infinitely many translates n+ℋn+\mathcal H containing at least two primes, and let H(k)H(k) be the least diameter hk−h1h_k-h_1 of an admissible kk-tuple. Then

DHL[k,2]⇒H1≤H(k),(1)\mathrm{DHL}[k,2]\ \Longrightarrow\ H_1\le H(k),\qquad(1)
and DHL[2,2]\mathrm{DHL}[2,2] applied to {0,2}\{0,2\} is the twin prime conjecture. All progress on H1H_1 since 2013 consists of proving DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] for smaller kk.

The bound 246246 stood from 2014 until the last days of August 2026. It has been formalised in Lean 4, assuming only the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem [1]. The four bounds below it in the table appeared within one week. They rest on Stadlmann’s idea of enlarging the support of the sieve weights using equidistribution estimates for moduli with a large smooth factor. The Lean development accompanying the bound 186186 is conditional on three stated inputs (two exponential-sum bounds and a family of numerical integral bounds) [12]. As of 7 September 2026 a standard reference still listed 246246 as the best fully established value [11]; on the date of this version we found no report of complete independent verification of the 2026 claims and no claimed bound below 186186. We therefore keep two columns throughout: established (246246) and claimed (186186).

Why 22 is a different problem

Under the generalised Elliott–Halberstam conjecture the sieve gives H1≤6H_1\le6, and Polymath8b showed that 66 cannot be improved by purely sieve-theoretic arguments: this is Selberg’s parity obstruction [13]. Lowering the bound (Track A below) and proving the conjecture (Track B) are therefore different tasks. No amount of success on Track A reaches 22.

What this project has verified for itself

The combinatorial half, checked by the Lean kernel

Our Lean development is written in core Lean 4, without Mathlib. It defines primality, the twin prime conjecture, the predicate BoundedGaps B (infinitely many pairs of distinct primes at distance at most BB), admissibility, and DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2]. It contains no sorry, and the only axioms it uses are propext and Quot.sound. The statement DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] is always a hypothesis, never an axiom.

The sieve constant, with exact certificates

Let ℛk={t∈[0,1]k:t1+…+tk≤1}\mathcal{R}_k=\{t\in[0,1]^k:\ t_1+\dots+t_k\le1\} and, for square-integrable FF supported on ℛk\mathcal{R}_k,

Ik(F)=∫ℛkF2,Jk(m)(F)=∫(∫Fdtm)2∏i≠mdti,Mk=supF∑m=1kJk(m)(F)Ik(F).I_k(F)=\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}F^2,\qquad J_k^{(m)}(F)=\int\left(\int F\,dt_m\right)^{2}\prod_{i\ne m}dt_i,\qquad M_k=\sup_F\frac{\sum_{m=1}^{k}J_k^{(m)}(F)}{I_k(F)}.
Maynard’s theorem [10, Prop. 4.2] states that if the primes have level of distribution θ\theta and θMk/2>1\theta M_k/2>1 then DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] holds. With Bombieri–Vinogradov (θ<1/2\theta<1/2) the condition is Mk>4M_k>4.

We take F=∑a+2b≤dca,b(1−P1)aP2bF=\sum_{a+2b\le d}c_{a,b}(1-P_1)^aP_2^b with Pj=∑itijP_j=\sum_it_i^j, as in [10, §8]. All integrals reduce to

∫ℛk(1−P1)AP2Bdt=A!(k+A+2B)!Gk(B),∑B≥0Gk(B)xBB!=(∑b≥0(2b)!b!xb)k,\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}(1-P_1)^AP_2^B\,dt=\frac{A!}{(k+A+2B)!}\,G_k(B),\qquad \sum_{B\ge0}G_k(B)\frac{x^B}{B!}=\left(\sum_{b\ge0}\frac{(2b)!}{b!}x^b\right)^{k},
which follows from the Dirichlet integral. The two quadratic forms are then exact rational matrices. We compute an approximate top eigenvector in floating point, round it to rationals, and evaluate the Rayleigh quotient in exact arithmetic; the last step is a rigorous lower bound whatever happened before it.

All symmetric test functions

To go further we allow every symmetric polynomial. Since P1,…,PkP_1,\dots,P_k generate the symmetric polynomials, the functions

(1−P1)aPb:=(1−P1)a∏j≥2Pjbj,a+w(b)≤d,w(b)=∑jjbj,(1-P_1)^a\,P^{b}:=(1-P_1)^a\prod_{j\ge2}P_j^{b_j},\qquad a+w(b)\le d,\quad w(b)=\sum_j jb_j,

where sb=∏jsjbjs^b=\prod_js_j^{b_j} and b!=∏jbj!b!=\prod_jb_j!. In particular each Ek(b)E_k(b) is a positive integer, computed exactly by multiplying exponential generating functions.

Proof. For a monomial, the Dirichlet integral gives ∫ℛk(1−P1)Atαdt=A!α!/(k+A+|α|)!\int_{\mathcal{R}_k}(1-P_1)^At^\alpha\,dt=A!\,\alpha!/(k+A+|\alpha|)!, and α!=𝔼Xα\alpha!=\mathbb EX^\alpha. The JJ-functional reduces to the same lemma in k−1k-1 variables: with Pj=Pj′+tkjP_j=P_j'+t_k^j,

∫01−P1′(1−P1)aPbdtk=∑e≤b(be)a!w(e)!(a+w(e)+1)!(1−P1′)a+w(e)+1P′b−e.\int_0^{1-P_1'}(1-P_1)^aP^b\,dt_k=\sum_{e\le b}\binom{b}{e} \frac{a!\,w(e)!}{(a+w(e)+1)!}\,(1-P_1')^{a+w(e)+1}P'^{\,b-e}.

Results of our own: rigidity of the pigeonhole step

Every bound above ends with the same step: if the weighted average number of primes among n+h1,…,n+hkn+h_1,\dots,n+h_k exceeds 11, two of them are prime, and they are at most hk−h1h_k-h_1 apart. A natural hope is to do better with unequal weights. Fix DD, and give each position a weight ci≥0c_i\ge0. Call a set of positions DD-separated if any two of its shifts differ by more than DD, and let αD(c)\alpha_D(c) be the largest total weight of a DD-separated set. If no two primes among the n+hin+h_i are within DD, then ∑ici𝟏n+hi prime≤αD(c)\sum_ic_i\mathbf 1_{n+h_i\text{ prime}}\le\alpha_D(c). Writing

Mk[c]=supF∑mcmJk(m)(F)Ik(F),M_k[c]=\sup_F\frac{\sum_{m}c_mJ_k^{(m)}(F)}{I_k(F)},
the sieve therefore yields H1≤DH_1\le D as soon as θ2Mk[c]>αD(c)\tfrac{\theta}{2}M_k[c]>\alpha_D(c). For c=(1,…,1)c=(1,\dots,1) and D=hk−h1D=h_k-h_1 this is the usual criterion. Could a cleverer cc certify some D<hk−h1D<h_k-h_1 that no sub-tuple certifies?

Proposition 6 (Proved here; possibly folklore). Let ω\omega be the largest number of shifts hih_i contained in an interval of length DD. Then for every c≥0c\ge0, not identically zero,

Mk[c]αD(c)≤Mω.\frac{M_k[c]}{\alpha_D(c)}\le M_\omega .
So the weighted criterion never certifies more than the standard criterion applied to the best sub-tuple of diameter at most DD.

Proof. Let GG be the graph on {1,…,k}\{1,\dots,k\} joining i≠ji\ne j when |hi−hj|≤D|h_i-h_j|\le D. Its stable sets are the DD-separated sets and its cliques are the sets of shifts lying in an interval of length DD. Normalise αD(c)=1\alpha_D(c)=1, so cc lies in

P={c≥0:c(S)≤1 for every stable set S}.P=\{c\ge0:\ c(S)\le1\text{ for every stable set }S\}.
The graph GG is a unit interval graph, hence perfect, and so is its complement by the perfect graph theorem [9]. For a perfect graph the polytope cut out by the clique inequalities is the convex hull of the indicator vectors of its stable sets [5]. Applying this to the complement of GG gives
P=conv{𝟏K:K a clique of G}.P=\operatorname{conv}\{\mathbf 1_K:\ K\text{ a clique of }G\}.

The map c↦Mk[c]c\mapsto M_k[c] is a supremum of linear functionals, hence convex, and therefore Mk[c]≤maxKMk[𝟏K]M_k[c]\le\max_KM_k[\mathbf 1_K]. It remains to show Mk[𝟏K]≤MjM_k[\mathbf 1_K]\le M_{j} for |K|=j|K|=j; this suffices because MjM_j is non-decreasing in jj (adjoin a variable and concentrate FF near its zero set). Write t=(s,u)t=(s,u) with ss the coordinates in KK and uu the others, put σ(u)=1−∑u≥0\sigma(u)=1-\sum u\ge0, and let Fu=F(⋅,u)F_u=F(\cdot,u), a function on the scaled simplex σ(u)ℛj\sigma(u)\mathcal{R}_j. For m∈Km\in K we have Jk(m)(F)=∫Jj(m)(Fu)duJ_k^{(m)}(F)=\int J_j^{(m)}(F_u)\,du, and Ik(F)=∫Ij(Fu)duI_k(F)=\int I_j(F_u)\,du. Rescaling FuF_u to ℛj\mathcal{R}_j multiplies IjI_j by σj\sigma^{j} and each Jj(m)J_j^{(m)} by σj+1\sigma^{j+1}, so

∑m∈KJj(m)(Fu)≤σ(u)MjIj(Fu)≤MjIj(Fu).\sum_{m\in K}J_j^{(m)}(F_u)\ \le\ \sigma(u)\,M_j\,I_j(F_u)\ \le\ M_j\,I_j(F_u).

The same rigidity on the enlarged simplex

Programme

Track A: lowering the bound

  1. Climb the ladder. Push the certified pipeline from k=56k=56 to M54>4M_{54}>4 (H1≤270H_1\le270; needs degree about 2020 to 2323), then add the ε\varepsilon-enlarged simplex (k=50k=50, 246246), then Stadlmann’s region (k=49k=49, 240240).

  2. Floor of the method (our main original direction). For the plain simplex the dual bound Mk≤kk−1logkM_k\le\frac{k}{k-1}\log k shows exactly where the method stops. No analogous upper bound is known to us for the 2026 hybrid supports. A rigorous dual certificate there would say whether 186186 is close to the limit of the present inputs or far from it, and which input (which Type I/II/III range) is binding.

  3. Which rung is cheap. From Table 1, the gain per unit of kk below the claimed frontier is 186→182→176→168→162→158186\to182\to176\to168\to162\to158 for k=40,…,35k=40,\dots,35: the steps 39→3839\to38 and 38→3738\to37 are worth the most.

Problem 11 (Open). Give a rigorous upper bound for the variational quantity of [16] as a function of kk and of the admissible exponents, and deduce the least kk the hybrid method can reach with the currently proved estimates.

Track B: the parity barrier

No sieve of the above kind can distinguish integers with an even number of prime factors from those with an odd number. Known ways around the barrier all inject bilinear or structural information. We will study, one per session, what each would need in order to say something about twin primes: Chen’s theorem [4]; Bombieri’s asymptotic sieve [2]; the parity-breaking sieve of Friedlander and Iwaniec [6]; Heath-Brown’s theorem that Siegel zeros would imply the conjecture [7]; the function-field proof of Sawin and Shusterman [14]; and the entropy-based results on correlations of multiplicative functions [17].

Problem 12 (Open, to be made precise in Lean). State, as a single explicit hypothesis on bilinear sums over n(n+2)n(n+2), the weakest known input that implies the twin prime conjecture, and formalise the implication.

Rules of this paper

A numerical bound enters the “established by this project” column only with an exact certificate and, for the combinatorial part, a Lean proof. Ideas are logged with their outcome, including failures. Nothing here is peer reviewed.

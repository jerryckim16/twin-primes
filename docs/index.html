The paper

Toward the twin prime conjecture: certificates, limits of the sieve, and a working programme

Jerry Kim

Version 0.4, 12 November 2026

This is a living working paper. It does not prove the twin prime conjecture, and it does not improve the best known bound on small gaps between primes. It records (i) the state of the problem as of the date above, (ii) what this project has verified for itself, by exact computation and by the Lean 4 kernel, now including the established bound H1≤246H_1\le246 of Polymath8b, reproduced end to end through a certified lower bound for M50,1/25M_{50,1/25}, (iii) small results of our own: a no-go statement for a natural attempt to sharpen the pigeonhole step, on the plain and on the ε\varepsilon-enlarged simplex; a generating-function formula that makes certified computation with all symmetric test functions cheap; an upper bound showing that the ε\varepsilon-enlargement of Polymath8b cannot, by itself, reach any k≤29k\le29; numerical evidence that this upper bound is close to the limit of every dual weight depending only on tmt_m and t1+…+tkt_1+\dots+t_k, and heuristic evidence that the ε\varepsilon-enlarged simplex stops at k=50k=50, and (iv) a programme of original directions that later versions will test. Every statement carries one of the labels Lean-checked, Computed, Proved here, Literature, Claimed (a preprint not yet independently verified), or Open.

The problem and the honest position

Write pnp_n for the nn-th prime and H1=liminfn→∞(pn+1−pn)H_1=\liminf_{n\to\infty}(p_{n+1}-p_n). The twin prime conjecture is the statement H1=2H_1=2. It is Open.

A finite set ℋ={h1<…<hk}\mathcal H=\{h_1<\dots<h_k\} of integers is admissible if for every prime pp it misses at least one residue class modulo pp. Let DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] be the statement that every admissible kk-tuple has infinitely many translates n+ℋn+\mathcal H containing at least two primes, and let H(k)H(k) be the least diameter hk−h1h_k-h_1 of an admissible kk-tuple. Then

DHL[k,2]⇒H1≤H(k),(1)\mathrm{DHL}[k,2]\ \Longrightarrow\ H_1\le H(k),\qquad(1)
and DHL[2,2]\mathrm{DHL}[2,2] applied to {0,2}\{0,2\} is the twin prime conjecture. All progress on H1H_1 since 2013 consists of proving DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] for smaller kk.

Why 22 is a different problem

Under the generalised Elliott–Halberstam conjecture the sieve gives H1≤6H_1\le6, and Polymath8b showed that 66 cannot be improved by purely sieve-theoretic arguments: this is Selberg’s parity obstruction [14]. Lowering the bound (Track A below) and proving the conjecture (Track B) are therefore different tasks. No amount of success on Track A reaches 22.

What this project has verified for itself

The combinatorial half, checked by the Lean kernel

Our Lean development is written in core Lean 4, without Mathlib. It defines primality, the twin prime conjecture, the predicate BoundedGaps B (infinitely many pairs of distinct primes at distance at most BB), admissibility, and DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2]. It contains no sorry, and the only axioms it uses are propext and Quot.sound. The statement DHL[k,2]\mathrm{DHL}[k,2] is always a hypothesis, never an axiom.

The sieve constant, with exact certificates

Results of our own: rigidity of the pigeonhole step

Every bound above ends with the same step: if the weighted average number of primes among n+h1,…,n+hkn+h_1,\dots,n+h_k exceeds 11, two of them are prime, and they are at most hk−h1h_k-h_1 apart. Problem 22 (Open). Give a rigorous upper bound for the variational quantity of [18] as a function of kk and of the admissible exponents, and deduce the least kk the hybrid method can reach with the currently proved estimates.

Track B: the parity barrier

No sieve of the above kind can distinguish integers with an even number of prime factors from those with an odd number. Known ways around the barrier all inject bilinear or structural information. We will study, one per session, what each would need in order to say something about twin primes: Chen’s theorem [4]; Bombieri’s asymptotic sieve [2]; the parity-breaking sieve of Friedlander and Iwaniec [6]; Heath-Brown’s theorem that Siegel zeros would imply the conjecture [7]; the function-field proof of Sawin and Shusterman [16]; and the entropy-based results on correlations of multiplicative functions [19].

Problem 23 (Open, to be made precise in Lean). State, as a single explicit hypothesis on bilinear sums over n(n+2)n(n+2), the weakest known input that implies the twin prime conjecture, and formalise the implication.

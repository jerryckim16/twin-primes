# Twin Prime Notebook

A working paper by **Jerry Kim** (prepared with the assistance of Claude) on small gaps between primes, toward the twin prime conjecture, with a Lean 4 development and exact computer certificates. Research sessions run three times a day (every eight hours); each one opens a pull request that is merged automatically, so the commit history is the research log.

**Website:** https://jerryckim16.github.io/twin-primes/

> No proof of the twin prime conjecture is claimed here. The conjecture is open, and the sieve methods behind every bound in this repository cannot reach 2 (the parity barrier). Nothing here has been peer reviewed.

## Small results

- [`parity-barrier`](papers/parity-barrier/main.tex) — *The parity barrier and twin primes: what is known and what is missing* (**v1.0.1, frozen**; v1.0 of 18 Nov 2026 plus a status note of 20 Nov on the Lü–Wang–Wu claim, 24 pp.; [PDF](https://jerryckim16.github.io/twin-primes/papers/parity-barrier.pdf)). A survey of the obstacle between bounded gaps and twin primes, read in the sources, with own results, each labelled: a window form of Tao's convex-hull criterion (Liouville-pattern weights block a proof of H<sub>m</sub> ≤ g exactly when g < H(2m+1), 6 for m = 1 — a statement about this weight class, not a bound); ceilings for Polymath8b's GEH criteria, the case k = 2 exactly at the parity value 2, and the k = 3 criterion above 2 at ε = 2/9, 1/4, 7/24 but provably below 2 for ε ≥ 0.439 (two programs each); Pintz's dichotomy with Maynard's weights (some even 0 < |d| ≤ 12 has λ(p+d) = −1 for infinitely many primes; Pintz: 16; possibly folklore); consistency tests (Chen's theorem); why Gowers norms and averages over the shift (MRT, Lichtman–Teräväinen, Fejér averages under RH + pair correlation) stop at or above the mean gap log X and never reach a fixed shift; the asymptotic sieves of Friedlander–Iwaniec and Bombieri for Λ(n+2); the bilinear structures behind a²+b⁴, x³+2y³ and F<sub>q</sub>[T]; the Siegel-zero world. Cut from 33 to 24 pages and frozen after a five-lane internal referee pass (`referee/2026-11-18-parity-barrier-v1/`). No claim of progress on the conjecture.
- [`verification-note`](papers/verification-note/main.tex) — *An independent exact verification of the variational step behind the claimed 2026 bounds H<sub>1</sub> ≤ 240 and H<sub>1</sub> ≤ 236* (**v1.0.1, frozen; erratum only**; [PDF](https://jerryckim16.github.io/twin-primes/papers/verification-note.pdf)): exact recomputation of the variational criteria of 240, 236 and 212 (Table 3 caps), a ceiling < 3.99 for the 212 draft's Lemma 9.1 caps (one-program Gram certificate, weights checked probabilistically against both programs), and checks of the 186 and 182 numerics, with a section saying for every number whether it rests on one program, two independent programs or a probabilistic check (after an internal referee pass). The analytic inputs are not verified.

Index: `papers/README.md`.

- Parity route cards since v1.0 (not in a paper): E2 pairs at a fixed difference d — in the linear-forms-with-multipliers model the parity obstruction survives every change of coordinates iff 4 ∤ d (2-adic colouring; [route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-18-e2-odd-cycles.md), Session 33). A statement about a heuristic weight class, not about twin primes. Green–Sawhney's Type II sums from Gowers norms (primes x²+ny² with x, y prime) cannot carry a fixed-shift twin condition: every placement is circular, lacks bilinear structure, or loses the two-dimensional averaging ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-19-green-sawhney-gowers-type-ii.md), Session 34; located, folklore in substance). Type II structure in the middle p+1 of a twin pair (smooth middles factor freely) cannot separate twins from the parity-fake weight unless bilinear two-point Chowla fails ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-19-smooth-middle-bilinear.md), Session 35; located, folklore in substance). The 2-adic no-go for E2 pairs at distance 2 extends from linear forms to polynomial parametrisations in several variables, including Chen-type variable multipliers x = u·L(m), except for a "forced vertex" case that is open (blocked in every case found) ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-19-e2-polynomial-gauge.md), Session 36; model-restricted, possibly folklore). For almost-prime k-tuples (Ω of a product of k linear forms at most r), the parity-pattern class forbids exactly r ≤ ⌊3k/2⌋ − 1: sharp at k = 2 (Chen's r = 3), while all published values for k ≥ 3, even under GEH (6, 8, 11, … for k = 3, 4, 5, …), lie well above it, so this class does not explain them ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-20-almost-prime-tuples.md), Session 37; corollary of the bipartite criterion, possibly folklore). Odd-order Chowla information (the parity-sensitive correlations proved by Tao–Teräväinen, if it could be used along sieve weights) changes nothing for any twin or bounded-gap target: within the class it blocks exactly the 2-colourable targets, against fractional chromatic number ≤ 2 without it, and every blocked gap target is 2-colourable; the smallest targets where it matters have 6 positions (others: the Fano plane, 7, Lean-checked; three primes in arithmetic progression among n + 210i, i < 9). Only the two-point input along the sieve weights (unweighted, Tao's 2016 theorem) would matter for twins ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-20-odd-order-chowla.md), Session 38; located, possibly folklore). Linnik's identity on rough pairs (n, n + 2) is a change of basis of the joint Ω-table, so the convolution-depth ladder stays inside the same class; the first missing step is a parity-sensitive asymptotic for one rough rung ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-21-linnik-ladder.md), Session 42; located, partly heuristic). Requiring every prime factor of p + 2 to be ≡ 1 mod q gives a sieve problem of dimension (q−2)/(q−1) < 1, solved under Elliott–Halberstam for each fixed q; its lower constant tends to 0 as the dimension tends to 1, the twin case, where it is exactly 0 (parity) ([route card](notes/parity-attempts/2026-11-22-dimension-ladder.md), Session 43; located, conditional, possibly folklore).

## How sessions work

Each session reads `STATE.md`, does one reproduction step and tests one idea of its own, updates the paper, the Lean code and the log, then pushes a session branch, opens a pull request and merges it. Failures are logged too.
